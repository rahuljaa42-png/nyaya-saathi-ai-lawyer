## Input

I resigned from a private software company in Hyderabad on 15 October 2026. They have not paid my October salary of Rs 42,000 or my full and final settlement. HR says 'it takes 90 days'. I have my offer letter and salary slips.

## Output

**What happened**
You resigned on 15 Oct 2026 from a private software company in Hyderabad. Your October salary of Rs 42,000 and the full‑and‑final settlement have not been paid. HR says it will take 90 days.

**Where you stand**
- Under the Code on Wages, 2019, wages for a monthly‑paid employee must be paid before the end of the 7th day of the next month [W-DUE].
- If you resign, your wages are due within two working days [W-EXIT].
Thus the employer is already in breach of the law.

**What to do next**
1. Send a **formal written reminder** (email or registered post) to the HR/management asking for payment of the October salary and the full‑and‑final settlement **within 2 working days** as required by law. Mention the statutory deadline and that you may file a wage claim if not paid.
2. If there is no response, **file a wage claim** with the labour authority (the officer not below Gazetted rank) in Hyderabad within **three years** of the claim arising [W-CLAIM].

**What to collect**
- Offer/appointment letter (you have).
- Salary slips (you have).
- Bank statement showing the last salary received.
- All email / message threads with HR about the pending salary and the 90‑day claim.
- Any resignation letter you submitted.
- Any written communication from the company (even a screenshot) stating the 90‑day timeline.

**When to get a real lawyer**
- If the employer refuses to pay even after your written reminder.
- If the labour authority’s process becomes complex or you need representation.
- If you want to claim interest or damages beyond the basic wage claim.

*This is general information, not legal advice.*

### When you need a real lawyer
- The other side replies through a lawyer, sends you a notice, or threatens you.
- You are near a time limit, or the amount is large for you.
- You are not sure the facts above are right. This is general information, not legal advice.
Free legal aid: call the NALSA helpline 15100 or visit your District Legal Services Authority. Women and children are eligible whatever they earn; others qualify under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, for example by income. [LA-NALSA]
