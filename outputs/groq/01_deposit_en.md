## Input

I moved out of my rented flat in Pune on 30 September 2026 and handed over the keys. The landlord took a deposit of Rs 50,000 by UPI and now says he will 'adjust it for painting' but won't give any bills. It has been almost two months. I have the rent agreement and WhatsApp chats.

## Output

**What happened**
You moved out of the flat in Pune on 30 Sept 2026, handed over the keys and the landlord kept your Rs 50,000 security deposit, saying it will be adjusted for painting but gave you no bills or written notice.

**Where you stand**
- Under the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 a residential security deposit can be at most two months’ rent and must be refunded when you give vacant possession, after deducting only genuine dues. [R-MTA]
- If your state (Maharashtra) has adopted the Model Tenancy Act, the landlord must show you a proper itemised bill for any deduction. Without such proof, the deduction is not “genuinely owed”.

**What to do next**
1. Ask the landlord in writing (WhatsApp/Email) for a detailed, item‑wise statement of the painting cost and the supporting bills. Mention that under the Model Tenancy Act a deduction must be backed by proof. 
2. If he does not reply within a reasonable time (say 7‑10 days), send a second notice stating that you will pursue recovery of the full deposit with interest.

**What to collect**
- Rent agreement (already have) 
- All WhatsApp chats with the landlord (already have) 
- Photos or video of the flat when you left (you plan to get) 
- Any receipts or bank/UPI proof of the deposit payment (you have) 
- Any written notice you received about deductions (if none, note that) 
- If possible, get a quotation for the painting work to show the cost is unreasonable.

**When to get a real lawyer**
- If the landlord refuses to give the itemised bill or still withholds the deposit after your written notice, you can file a civil suit for recovery. The suit must be filed within about three years of the dispute. [R-SUIT]
- If you are unsure whether Maharashtra has adopted the Model Tenancy Act, or need help drafting the notice, consult a lawyer or free legal aid.

*This is general information, not legal advice.*
