### Where you stand and what to do
1. **Collect your proof.** Order ID, invoice, payment proof and screenshots of the refund status.
2. **Write to the Grievance Officer.** Use the platform's Grievance Officer contact (usually in the footer or 'Grievance' page). Keep the complaint number.
    - Every e-commerce platform must have a Grievance Officer whose name and contact are shown on the platform. Complaints must be acknowledged within 48 hours and resolved within one month. [CP-ECOM]
3. **Register on the National Consumer Helpline.** Free, and companies often respond to it.
    - Before going to the Commission you can register a free grievance with the National Consumer Helpline: call 1915 or 1800-11-4000, or use consumerhelpline.gov.in. It works in 17 languages. [CP-NCH]
4. **File at the District Consumer Commission.** If there is still no refund, file online.
    - Consumer complaints can be filed online on the e-Jagriti portal (e-jagriti.gov.in), which replaced e-Daakhil and has been available in consumer commissions across the country since 1 January 2025. [CP-EJAGRITI]
    - Consumer complaints worth up to ₹50 lakh go to the District Consumer Commission. The value is what you paid for the goods or service, not the compensation you ask for. [CP-FORUM]
    - At the District Commission there is no filing fee for claims up to ₹5 lakh. Check the current fee on the e-Jagriti portal before filing. _(Secondary source; confirm on the portal.)_ [CP-FEE]
    - A consumer complaint must be filed within two years of the problem (the 'cause of action'). [CP-LIMIT]
Free legal aid: call the NALSA helpline 15100 or visit your District Legal Services Authority. Women and children are eligible whatever they earn; others qualify under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, for example by income. [LA-NALSA]
