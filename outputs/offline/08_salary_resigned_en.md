### Where you stand and what to do
1. **Collect your proof.** Offer letter, salary slips, bank statements and messages asking for the salary.
2. **Send a written demand to HR.** Send the demand letter below by email, with the exact months and amount.
    - Salary disputes are now governed by the Code on Wages, 2019. Its main provisions came into force on 21 November 2025 and it replaced the Payment of Wages Act, 1936. [W-CODE]
    - For monthly-paid employees, wages must be paid before the end of the 7th day of the next month. [W-DUE]
    - If you resigned, or were removed, dismissed or retrenched, your wages are due within two working days. _(The state government can set a different time limit; check your state's rules.)_ [W-EXIT]
3. **File a wage claim.** If unpaid, file a claim with the wage authority (ask your district labour office who it is).
    - You can file a wage claim before the authority appointed under the Code (an officer not below Gazetted rank), within three years of the claim arising. A registered trade union or the Inspector-cum-Facilitator can also file for you. Late claims can be accepted if you show sufficient cause. [W-CLAIM]
    - Some central and state rules under the new labour codes were still being notified after the codes started, so who exactly the claim authority is in your state should be confirmed with your local labour office. [W-RULES]
4. **Get free help.** Free legal aid can help you file.
    - Free legal aid: call the NALSA helpline 15100 or visit your District Legal Services Authority. Women and children are eligible whatever they earn; others qualify under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, for example by income. [LA-NALSA]
